\section{Introduction}

Thermal inspection of packaged or partially exposed chips often needs clearer
internal contour visibility than a single long-wave infrared frame can provide.
The current detector samples the scene at 10 um per pixel, but the calibrated
spatial resolution is about 20 um and the LWIR optical transfer function
attenuates fine thermal structure. The practical question is therefore not
whether a 5 um temperature metrology image can be recovered, but whether a
2x reconstruction grid can make chip contours more stable and interpretable.

This project studies a step-and-shoot micro-scan session with 255 usable TXT
temperature frames. The contribution is an experimental pipeline for bounded
thermal super-resolution:

\begin{itemize}
\item session-aware data selection that avoids cross-temperature mixing;
\item data-driven micro-scan alignment that treats stage commands and filenames
as priors rather than ground truth;
\item a 2x contour-level reconstruction comparison across SAA, IBP, and MAP-TV;
\item alignment and regularization ablations that expose when sharper images
are caused by alignment residuals rather than supported structure.
\end{itemize}

The headline result is deliberately conservative. Data-driven alignment is
essential, but a better held-out contour score does not automatically make a
forward-model SR algorithm reliable. In the current evidence, default contour
refined alignment remains the main EP06 choice, while tuned alignment and NCC
initialization are sensitivity controls.
