\section{Limitations and Conclusion}

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Single instrument.} All real data come from one calibrated LWIR
system and one chip family (248 clean frames in the main session). The protocol
and null-space diagnostics are designed to transfer, but external validity is
currently argued rather than demonstrated as a broad benchmark.

\item \textbf{No external high-resolution anchor.} We do not have an
independent high-resolution thermal reference for the exact same scene. Our
``trustworthy'' claim therefore means consistency under internal controls
(FRC-based controls, observation anchoring, and structure proxies), not
agreement with a held-out ground truth.

\item \textbf{Partial FRC-control instability.} Positive/negative controls around
the 10--12\,$\mu$m rebound are not ideal. We mitigate this by making the
17\,$\mu$m cutoff the main claim and explicitly marking the rebound range as a
potential artifact-risk region.

\item \textbf{Proxy ceiling.} Artifact/correlation proxies and split-half
consistency can guard against hallucination, but they cannot certify every
recovered fine detail. Our acceptance rule is intentionally conservative and may
reject reconstructions that are useful but not certifiable.

\item \textbf{PSF uncertainty and finest structures.} The plausible PSF range
($\sigma\in[0.2,0.5]$ LR pixels) is bounded but not pinned to a single value,
and all PSF-dependent conclusions are reported with this interval. At 10\,$\mu$m
pitch, 1--2 pixel structures are near the physical resolution boundary; the
deliverable is contour visibility and stability, not literal recovery.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Conclusion}

We study burst super-resolution at a deliberately hard operating point: an
industrial LWIR micro-scan system with no high-resolution ground truth at any
stage. Under a fully measured forward model, we show that (i) the burst carries
real but bounded coherent information beyond a single LR frame, (ii) standard
$1\times$ statistical inputs can hide subpixel phase evidence that becomes
visible under evidence injection, and (iii) synthetic-prior-trained networks can
drift along the observation operator null space while forward-consistency loss
stays near floor.

Classical reconstruction anchors (coverage-weighted TGV and fine-grid MAP-TV)
remain the most faithful to verifiable observations, while learning-based
variants can produce richer-looking details together with more unverifiable
high-frequency content. In this ground-truth-free region, we therefore do not
certify a single winner. Instead, we report verifiable fidelity proxies, a
structure-grain proxy with dual-domain visual gates, and a task-level contour
preference that survives explicit controls.

The resulting claim is intentionally bounded: controlled contour-level
$2\times$ enhancement, with transparent reporting of what survives controls and
what does not.
