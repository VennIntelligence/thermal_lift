\begin{abstract}
Long-wave infrared chip inspection is limited by optical resolution, thermal
noise, and imperfect micro-scan motion. This paper skeleton organizes the
current Thermal Lift evidence into a reproducible 2x contour-level
super-resolution proof of concept. The central finding is that filename or
stage-command motion is only a prior: data-driven contour alignment gives more
accurate frame registration and changes the interpretation of forward-model
methods. On the 255-frame main session, shift-and-add provides the most stable
baseline, iterative back-projection is useful as a forward-model stress test,
and MAP-TV becomes a conservative regularized diagnostic rather than the
sharpest or best method. The result is a bounded contour-visibility claim, not
a 4x or 5 um metrology claim.
\end{abstract}
