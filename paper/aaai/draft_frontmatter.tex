\begin{abstract}
Long-wave infrared (LWIR) thermography supports semiconductor inspection by measuring sub-degree
temperature patterns from defects and process variations. In this setting, a sharper-looking
reconstruction does not imply higher true resolution, so visual sharpness cannot by itself indicate
successful super-resolution. The setting is constrained by physics and data: diffraction suppresses
out-of-band information, higher-end optics are costly, and paired high-resolution thermal
measurements are unavailable. We study a calibrated 2$\times$ micro-scan burst-SR system through three
questions: whether the burst contains recoverable high-frequency information, whether learned
models actually receive it, and whether added detail remains anchored to the observations.

Our main finding is \textbf{null-space drift}. We model imaging as an observation operator $A$ that maps a
hypothetical high-resolution temperature field to the measured low-resolution frames through
motion, optical blur, detector integration, and downsampling. The operator has a null space:
high-frequency changes that alter the reconstruction while producing nearly the same measured
frames. When networks trained on physics-matched synthetic data are applied to real data, their
added details drift primarily in this null space. The evidence is diagnostic: the
forward-consistency loss---the mismatch after passing the reconstruction back through $A$---stays at the
noise/model-error floor, while ground-truth-free artifact and correlation diagnostics on real data
continue to worsen. We therefore use a ground-truth-free evaluation protocol combining controlled
split-half consistency, observation-side drift diagnostics, and pre-specified checkpoint selection.
The conclusion is bounded: classical variational reconstruction is most faithful to verifiable
observations, whereas evidence-injected learning gives sharper visible contours but more
unverifiable high-frequency content; without high-resolution thermal ground truth, neither is
certifiably dominant.
\end{abstract}
